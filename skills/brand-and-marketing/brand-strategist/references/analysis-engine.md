# Phase 1 — The Analysis Engine

How to read a brand honestly and compactly. Output stays compressed (see
graphify.md); this file is the *method* behind the read.

## The read protocol

Work through whatever sources exist. Fetch live URLs/handles when tools allow,
otherwise use Monny's supplied screenshots, exports, and notes.

### Per-platform checklist

**Website / link-in-bio**
- Does the first screen state a claim or just describe?
- Is there a clear "who this is for"?
- Does the visual language match the rest of the ecosystem?

**Instagram**
- Bio: claim vs. description. Is the hidden asset hiding here?
- Grid cohesion: palette, type, treatment consistency.
- Highlights: structured or random?
- Top posts: what performs, and what does that reveal about the real audience?

**TikTok**
- Hook discipline in the first 2 seconds.
- Is there a repeatable format or is every video different?
- Live vs. posted content balance (relevant for sellers).

**LinkedIn**
- Positioning line under the name.
- Is the content authority-building or just activity?

**YouTube / long-form**
- Is there a content architecture or one-off uploads?

### Cross-platform questions
- Could a stranger say what this person is about in 5 seconds?
- Is the same person showing up across platforms, or three different brands?
- What is the cadence — consistent, erratic, or dormant?

### The hidden asset hunt (do this every time)
Find the line, phrase, or angle already present but underused. Examples from real
engagements:
- Kimmie's bio "carefully selected to suit your body type and size" -> the entire
  positioning around body-type-aware inclusive curation.
- A throwaway phrase in someone's captions that is actually their tagline.

The hidden asset is frequently the whole strategy, sitting unclaimed. Surface it
loudly in the Phase 1 deliverable.

## The Brand Scorecard rubric

Score each dimension 0-100. Be honest. A flattering read is worthless; an honest
read builds trust and justifies the intervention. Anchor your scores:

**Strategic Alignment** — stands for something specific?
- 0-40: generic, could be anyone in the category
- 41-70: a direction exists but it's fuzzy
- 71-100: a clear, specific, defensible stance

**Visual Cohesion** — consistent, intentional visual language?
- 0-40: inconsistent, ad hoc, template-default
- 41-70: some consistency, no system
- 71-100: a recognizable, intentional system

**Market Disruptiveness** — owns a distinct position vs. peers?
- 0-40: blends in, follows category norms
- 41-70: some differentiation, not owned
- 71-100: owns a distinct, hard-to-copy position

**Messaging Clarity** — audience can repeat the point?
- 0-40: unclear what they're even saying
- 41-70: understandable but not memorable
- 71-100: sticky, repeatable, one clear point

**Expression Confidence** — shows up boldly and consistently?
- 0-40: hesitant, sporadic, hiding
- 41-70: present but inconsistent
- 71-100: bold, consistent, unmistakable

Overall = average of the five. Render via the scorecard + radar in graphify.md.

## The Gaps Table

Most brands have 3 core gaps, occasionally up to 5. The classic triad:

| Gap | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Positioning** | No specific claim; trying to appeal to everyone |
| **Language** | Borrowed/generic voice; doesn't sound like them |
| **Visibility** | Inconsistent or absent; not showing up |

But always read the *actual* brand — gaps may be: audience confusion, offer
ladder missing, visual incoherence, no content system, authority not built, etc.

Each row of the delivered table:

| Gap | Evidence (specific, what you saw) | The fix (Phase 3 hook) |
|---|---|---|

Evidence must be concrete and specific to this brand. The fix column is the bridge
into the strategy phase — it previews what the build will resolve.

---

## Phase 1 deliverable structure (the Analysis Document)

Landscape. Compact. In order:
1. Cover / eyebrow with client name + phase metadata
2. Snapshot: 4-6 one-line observations
3. Brand Scorecard (5 dimensions + overall) with radar
4. The hidden asset (called out as a feature)
5. Gaps Table (3, up to 5 rows)
6. The 3 biggest opportunities
7. What happens next (-> discovery questionnaire)

This is the "where we are" milestone. It is honest, premium, and sets up Phase 2.
