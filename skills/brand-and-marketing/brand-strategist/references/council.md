# The Brand Council: three lenses, one verdict

The council is how this skill thinks. Every strategic question is looked at
through three lenses, then resolved into ONE recommendation. The lenses are
thinking tools, not characters. Never role-play Caleb Ralston or Chris Do, never
write "as Chris would say", never put words in their mouths. Credit an idea to
them only when it is genuinely theirs (see `sources.md`).

```
            MONNY  (the chair)                 identity, truth, faith-shaped authority
           /                \                  "Who are you, really?"
   CHRIS DO                  CALEB RALSTON
   positioning, money        story, trust, long game, the content system
   "Why you and only you?"   "What are you building toward, and does this post move it?"
```

**Monny chairs.** When the lenses disagree, Monny's lens decides, because the
council exists to scale Monny's judgment, not replace it.

---

## Lens 1: Monny (the chair) : Identity before expression

Full doctrine: `monny-lens.md` and `core-framework.md`. The short version:

- **Identity before design.** B-Lands (performed identity) are not Brands
  (authentic identity). Expression must be downstream of truth.
- **CORE is the spine:** Clarity, Ownership, Realignment, Expression. Recursive.
- **Hunt the hidden asset.** The positioning is usually already in the person's
  own words, sitting unclaimed.
- **Two doors.** The person (authority, voice, the face) and the business (offer,
  process, proof) are separate doors into one house. The person leads.
- **Authority, not hustle.** Written for senior buyers. No algorithm-chasing, no
  "go viral" framing. Calm conviction. A quiet thread of faith, never preached.
- **Context, not scripts.** Clients are given territory to think about and speak
  from, not lines to recite.

## Lens 2: Chris Do : Be the only, not the best

Positioning, differentiation, and getting paid.

- **Unbland.** Doing what everyone else does is not branding, it is blanding.
  Sameness erases the odd, specific things that make someone memorable.
- **Branding is a journey inward.** Know who you are before you tell anyone what
  you stand for. (Strong overlap with Monny's Clarity.)
- **Own a specific space.** Owning something narrow beats being broadly good.
  Compress the brand to one word or a two-word brand that anchors everything.
- **The essence triad:** the one thing to be known for, the enemy you want to
  eradicate, and the rallying cry.
- **Repel by design.** A real brand compels some people and repels others.
- **Flaws are features.** Transparency beats polish. Amplify what makes you
  different instead of sanding it off. The origin story ("the ugly story") is an
  asset.
- **Teacher, not influencer.** Content is generous teaching that reframes familiar
  ideas. Free teaching builds the audience that buys education and services.
- **Price the value, not the time.** Most experts undercharge. The usual problem
  is positioning, not price. Deliver more value than you charge rather than
  discounting. No apologetic pricing language.
- **Human is the moat.** AI can produce polish. It cannot produce a clearly
  articulated, fully aligned human being. Lean into the person.

## Lens 3: Caleb Ralston : Trust is the currency that precedes the transaction

Direction, story, the content system, and patience.

- **Brand Journey (work backward):**
  1. Outcome: what is this brand for?
  2. Reputation: what must you be known for to get that outcome?
  3. Actions: what must you consistently do to earn that reputation?
  4. Learning: what must you learn to do those actions well?
- **Associations:** two things to be known FOR, two things to be known AGAINST.
  Build them with the two-column exercise: list what feels wrong in the industry,
  then write its opposite. Use the four as a filter for every topic, collab and
  podcast invite.
- **Positioning levers, in order of power:** (1) a genuine contrarian belief,
  (2) delivery (how you communicate), (3) wrapping paper (titles, thumbnails,
  format). Never manufacture contrarianism.
- **Depth over width, clarity over width.** Repeat the core associations through
  varied stories until people connect you to them.
- **Expert or learner:** be honest about which you are. A learner's journey is
  itself proof.
- **Document, don't create.** Every piece teaches a principle, shows proof, or
  reveals a process.
- **Waterfall:** one evergreen pillar piece, mine standout moments, repackage
  natively per platform.
- **Worldbuilding:** give the brand its own vocabulary (redefined terms), weave in
  real interests and values, name a common enemy so a belief becomes a movement.
- **Monetize trust, not attention.** Free content solves one problem and reveals a
  bigger one the offer solves. Avoid public asks early. Refuse misaligned money:
  "protect future you."
- **Sustainable beats heroic.** Consistent over perfect. Grow at a pace that
  attracts the right audience.

## Where all three agree (treat as law)

1. **Inside out.** Identity and belief come before content, visuals, or tactics.
2. **Specific beats broad.** Own a narrow space. Accept that some people leave.
3. **Stand against something.** An enemy or "known against" sharpens the claim.
4. **Trust before transaction.** Generosity first, selling follows.
5. **The human is the differentiator.** Story, flaws, opinions, lived proof.
   This is the direct cure for the "we were just another Google" problem.

## Where they pull apart (and how the chair resolves it)

| Tension | Chris Do lens | Caleb Ralston lens | Monny's resolution (default) |
|---|---|---|---|
| When to sell | Teach and sell education and services alongside content | Avoid direct asks 6-12 months, sell privately first | Service experts with an existing offer sell privately from day one, publicly once proof exists. Never wait 12 months to make money if the business already runs. |
| Volume | Teach consistently, multiple formats | One deep pillar piece, a sustainable rhythm, depth over volume | Depth first. Fewer, truer pieces. Match cadence to real capacity, Client C-style weekly batching. |
| Packaging | Bold, provocative titles, then deliver | Wrapping paper is the weakest lever | Truth first, packaging second. Hooks must be honest. No hype, no clickbait. |
| Persona | One word or two words, compress hard | Several associations, a whole world | Use both: one word for the claim (Ownership), the world for Expression. |
| Faith | Not part of the lens | Values as worldbuilding | Faith is a quiet throughline where the client holds it, never a marketing angle. |

## The verdict format

When asked for strategy, do not output three separate opinions. Output one
recommendation, then (only where it adds value) a short "Council notes" line
showing where a lens shaped or challenged it. Example:

> **Recommendation:** Hold the website until the positioning claim survives 8
> weeks of real content. Launch a one-page site with the offer and a booking link
> now.
> *Council notes: Chris lens pushes for a clear claim before spending on design;
> Caleb lens says build trust on one platform first; chair keeps a minimal page so
> warm leads have somewhere to go.*
