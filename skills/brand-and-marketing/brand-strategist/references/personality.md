# Personality and Point of View: "Don't be another Google"

The most common failure in expert content, and the one Monny diagnosed in his
own LinkedIn and videos: posting information anyone could search. If the content
does not need the person, the audience does not need the person.

## The Google Test (run on every idea, every script brief, every post)

Ask: **"Could someone get this exact thing from a Google search or a chatbot?"**

- **Yes** → it is a commodity. Do not post it as is. Add at least one of the five
  human layers below.
- **No** → it carries the person. Proceed.

### The five human layers (add at least one, ideally two)

| Layer | What it adds | Prompt to ask the person |
|---|---|---|
| **Story** | Lived experience | "When did this happen to you or a client? What did it cost?" |
| **Opinion** | A stance, including against something | "What does your industry get wrong about this?" |
| **Proof** | Real numbers, real results, real process | "Show the work: before, after, the messy middle." |
| **Framework** | Their own named way of thinking | "Do you have a name for how you do this?" |
| **Taste** | What they would choose and why | "Of all the options, which one would you pick and why?" |

Rewrite example:
- Commodity: "5 tips for building a personal brand on LinkedIn."
- Carries the person: "Our LinkedIn was a library nobody visited. Here is the one
  question that fixed it."

## Building the personality system (for a client or for Monny)

Work through these in order. Each one comes from the council (see `council.md`).

1. **The one word / two words** (Chris Do lens, Monny's Ownership). The word the
   brand owns. Test: can a stranger repeat it after three posts?
2. **Known FOR (x2) and known AGAINST (x2)** (Caleb Ralston lens). Use the
   two-column exercise: what feels wrong in the industry, then its opposite.
3. **The contrarian belief.** What do they genuinely believe about their industry
   that their peers do not? Must be true for them, never manufactured.
4. **The enemy and the rallying cry.** The thing they want gone, and the line their
   people would chant. (Illustration from the Client A build: her signature
   line "As a believer, your life does not have to be boring." works as a rallying
   cry, and it implies the enemy, a dull faith that never touches real life.)
5. **Delivery.** How they communicate: calm teacher, warm pastor, sharp operator,
   playful storyteller. Pick what is natural, not what is trending.
6. **The world.** Their vocabulary (terms they redefine or coin), recurring
   interests and values that show up in content, signature phrases, rituals.
7. **Flaws and the ugly story.** The origin story, including the part that is not
   polished. Transparency is the trust engine. (Respect privacy: the client
   chooses what is ready. Never probe a story they have closed.)
8. **Expert or learner.** Be honest about which. Both work, faking either fails.

Output of this module: a one-page **Personality Card**:

```
ONE WORD:        ...
KNOWN FOR:       ... / ...
KNOWN AGAINST:   ... / ...
BELIEF:          "..."
ENEMY:           ...
RALLYING CRY:    "..."
DELIVERY:        ...
VOCABULARY:      term = meaning, term = meaning
WORLD:           recurring interests, values, rituals
STORY BEATS:     origin, turning point, the scar, the now
MODE:            expert | learner
```

The Personality Card is the filter for every content brief in
`content-engine.md`.

## Unique value proposition (short form)

Write it as: **"I help [one specific person] [get one specific result] through
[their named method or angle], unlike [the enemy / the common approach]."**
Then test it with the Google Test: if a competitor could paste the same sentence
into their bio, it is not unique yet. Push the angle or the method until it isn't.
